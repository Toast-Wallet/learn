## 💡 Short answer

Fake Toast Wallet support agents contact you first, act friendly and urgent, and eventually ask for your words, passphrase, backup code, secret key or remote access. Real help never needs any of these. Use the guides in the [Toast Wallet learn hub](https://github.com/Toast-Wallet/learn) and report app problems publicly in the [Toast Wallet app repository](https://github.com/Toast-Wallet/core).

## 👀 You'll see this if

- You posted about a wallet problem and received private messages offering help.
- Someone claims to be "Toast Wallet support" on social media, email or a messaging app.
- A helper asks you to install a screen-sharing app.
- You are told your wallet must be "validated", "synchronised" or "unlocked".

## 🤔 Why it happens

Asking for help in public is natural, but scammers monitor forums, comment sections and social media for exactly those posts. Within minutes, they reply with offers of assistance, often using names and profile pictures that look official. Some set up fake help desks with ticket numbers to seem legitimate.

Their script is predictable. First they sympathise. Then they invent a technical reason for your problem. Finally they ask for your secrets or for remote access to "fix" it. Once they have either, they empty your wallet. Because XRP payments are final, the money cannot be reversed.

## 🧭 Before you start

Remember three facts. Toast Wallet does not run a private-message support desk. No one needs your secrets to help you. And any request for remote access to a computer holding a wallet is a red flag.

## 🛠️ Fix: handle "support" contact safely

1. **Do not reply to private offers.** Real projects do not contact you first.
2. **Never share secrets.** That includes the six words, passphrase, backup code and any key starting with `s`.
3. **Never install remote-access software** at someone else's request while your wallet is on the computer.
4. **Use public, official resources.** Read the relevant guide in the learn hub, for example [Toast Wallet forgot passphrase](https://github.com/Toast-Wallet/learn/blob/main/how-to/toast-wallet-forgot-passphrase.md), or open a public issue in the app repository without including any secret.
5. **Block and report** the account on the platform where it contacted you.
6. **If you already shared secrets,** create a new wallet on a clean computer, back it up and move your XRP immediately.

## 🧩 Real-world example

A user posts on a social network: "My Toast Wallet passphrase is not working." Five accounts reply within minutes, one called "Toast Wallet Help Desk", inviting them to a private chat. The "agent" says the wallet needs re-synchronising and sends a link to a "sync portal" asking for the backup code and six words. The user recognises the pattern from this guide, blocks the account and follows the passphrase troubleshooting steps instead. They get back in on their own.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes

- Trusting an account because its name or logo includes "Toast".
- Believing ticket numbers and formal language mean an agent is real.
- Allowing remote access "just to look".
- Paying an upfront "recovery fee".

## 🔁 If that did not work

- **You gave remote access.** Disconnect the computer from the internet, uninstall the remote-access software and, from a different clean device, set up a new wallet and move your XRP.
- **You paid a recovery fee.** That money is unlikely to return. Do not pay any further "release" fees.
- **You are unsure if a message is genuine.** If it asks for secrets, access or money, it is not. See [Toast Wallet phishing sites warning](https://github.com/Toast-Wallet/learn/blob/main/how-to/toast-wallet-phishing-sites-warning.md).

## 🛡️ Prevention

When asking for help publicly, never mention how much XRP you hold. Avoid posting screenshots that show addresses with large balances. Keep your questions general and rely on the guides. Review [Toast Wallet scams to avoid](https://github.com/Toast-Wallet/learn/blob/main/how-to/toast-wallet-scams-to-avoid.md) with family members who might help you.

## 📝 Good to know

Fake support scams work because they arrive at a vulnerable moment, when you are already worried about your XRP. Recognising that emotional pressure is part of the defence. A real solution can wait an hour while you read a guide. A scammer will push you to act now. Whenever you feel rushed, stop, close the conversation and come back later with a clear head.

## 🔍 A closer look at where real help lives

Real help for Toast Wallet is public and passive. The guides in the learn hub are written so you can solve common problems yourself, at your own pace. Bugs and questions about the app itself can be raised publicly in the GitHub repository, where anyone can see the conversation and where nobody needs your secrets. No genuine helper will move the conversation to a private chat and then ask you for anything sensitive.

When you need to ask a question, describe the symptom and your system, for example "Windows 11, Toast Wallet 3.0.0, passphrase refused when sending". That gives enough information for a useful answer. Leave out balances, addresses with large holdings and anything secret. If a reply asks you to share more privately, treat that as the moment to stop.

Keep a note of this rule somewhere visible near your backup materials, so it is the first thing you see whenever you are tempted to ask a stranger for help with your wallet.

## ❓ FAQ

<details>
<summary>Does Toast Wallet have a support phone line?</summary>

No phone or chat support exists that needs your secrets. Treat any such offer as a scam.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Is it safe to open a GitHub issue?</summary>

Yes, as long as you never include words, passphrases, backup codes or keys.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Can a real agent see my wallet remotely?</summary>

No one legitimate asks to. Remote access to a wallet computer is a red flag.

</details>
